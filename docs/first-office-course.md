 Fluent — First Course: Detailed Curriculum

This document covers the full lesson-by-lesson breakdown of everything in the MVP: the Office/Admin Quick-Start, all four Fundamentals modules, and the AI for Office Work role track.

Each lesson follows the micro-design format: **Concept (60-90s) → See It (60s) → Try It (90-120s) → Lock It (30-45s)**. Total time per lesson: ~4-5 minutes.

---

## QUICK-START: "AI for Your Workday" (Office/Admin)

**Purpose:** Before any teaching happens, show the user AI doing something immediately useful for their actual life. This is the hook. They should finish this and think "okay, I need to learn how to do more of this."

### QS-1: "Let AI Handle the Email You've Been Avoiding"
- **Concept:** You can paste a difficult email into AI and ask it to draft a reply. It takes 30 seconds.
- **See It:** A real example — an annoying email from a vendor, pasted into ChatGPT/Claude, with the prompt "Reply professionally declining this but keeping the relationship warm." Show the output.
- **Try It:** The user is given a sample difficult email (scheduling conflict with a client). Write a prompt to get a good reply. App shows the AI output based on their prompt.
- **Lock It:** "Which of these prompts would give you a better reply?" (two options, pick the better one)
- **XP:** 15 XP (bonus for first-ever lesson)

### QS-2: "Summarize That Document Nobody Read"
- **Concept:** You can feed AI a long document and get the 3 key takeaways in seconds.
- **See It:** A 4-page meeting recap → pasted into AI → 3-bullet summary shown.
- **Try It:** Given a short dense paragraph (a company policy update), write a prompt to summarize it. Compare their result to an expert prompt's result.
- **Lock It:** "What should you include in a summarization prompt to get better results?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### QS-3: "Turn Your Messy Notes into Something Useful"
- **Concept:** AI can take scattered bullet points and turn them into structured documents, action items, or formatted emails.
- **See It:** Raw meeting notes (messy, abbreviated) → prompt → clean action item list with owners and deadlines.
- **Try It:** Given messy notes from a brainstorming session, write a prompt to organize them. App evaluates the prompt and shows the output.
- **Lock It:** "What format should you ask for when you want AI to organize chaotic notes?" (pick best answer)
- **XP:** 10 XP

**Quick-Start complete.** Total time: ~12-15 minutes. The user has now used AI (or simulated using it) three times for real tasks. The app celebrates: "You just used AI for 3 real work tasks. Ready to understand how this actually works?" → Leads into Fundamentals.

## FUNDAMENTALS MODULE F1: "What Actually Exists"

**Goal:** Give the user a mental map of the AI landscape so they stop feeling lost. Not comprehensive — just enough to orient. 6 lessons.

### F1-1: "AI Is Not One Thing"
- **Concept:** When people say "AI" they usually mean one of three things: chatbots (talk to AI), copilots (AI assists while you work), or agents (AI does tasks on its own). These are very different.
- **See It:** Visual showing the three categories with a real example of each — ChatGPT (chatbot), GitHub Copilot (copilot), a booking agent (agent).
- **Try It:** Match exercise — 6 AI products, sort them into chatbot / copilot / agent.
- **Lock It:** "Your colleague says 'I used AI to book my travel automatically.' Which type of AI is this?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F1-2: "The Big Players"
- **Concept:** There are a few major companies making the AI you actually use: OpenAI (ChatGPT), Anthropic (Claude), Google (Gemini), Meta (open source models). Each has a personality and strengths.
- **See It:** Side-by-side comparison card — same prompt, different outputs from ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. Brief note on each company's vibe.
- **Try It:** "Based on these descriptions, which tool would you try first for a long research report?" (scenario-based pick)
- **Lock It:** Match the company to their main product.
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F1-3: "What AI Can and Can't Do (Honestly)"
- **Concept:** AI is great at drafting, summarizing, brainstorming, and translating between formats. It's bad at facts, math, real-time info, and anything requiring true understanding. Knowing the limits saves you from embarrassment.
- **See It:** Two examples — one where AI nails it (rewriting a paragraph in a different tone) and one where it fails (confidently giving a wrong statistic).
- **Try It:** "Spot the hallucination" — read an AI-generated paragraph about a real topic, find the made-up fact.
- **Lock It:** "Which of these tasks should you NOT trust AI to do without checking?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F1-4: "Free vs. Paid — What You Get"
- **Concept:** Most AI tools have a free tier that's useful and a paid tier that's better. The main differences: speed, model quality, file handling, and limits. You don't need to pay to start.
- **See It:** Comparison table — ChatGPT free vs Plus, Claude free vs Pro. What actually changes.
- **Try It:** "Your boss wants you to summarize 50 pages of reports using AI. Would the free tier work, or do you need paid? Why?" (scenario quiz)
- **Lock It:** "What's the main reason to upgrade from free to paid AI tools?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F1-5: "AI on Your Phone, Desktop, and Browser"
- **Concept:** AI isn't just a website you visit. It's in your phone (Siri/Google Assistant are getting smarter), your browser (extensions), your desktop (Copilot in Windows, Apple Intelligence), and your work tools (Notion AI, Grammarly). You're probably already using it without knowing.
- **See It:** Visual map of where AI lives across a typical person's devices and tools.
- **Try It:** Checklist exercise — "Which of these AI-powered features have you used without realizing it?" (check all that apply, then reveal what they are)
- **Lock It:** "Which of these is NOT an example of AI already in your daily tools?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F1-6: "Your AI Starting Point"
- **Concept:** You don't need to learn everything. Based on what you do and what you want, here's your recommended next step. This is a personalized checkpoint, not a traditional lesson.
- **See It:** A "Your AI Profile" card showing: tools they've tried, what they said they do, their comfort level. Generated from onboarding + quick-start performance.
- **Try It:** "Set one AI goal for this week" — pick from a list of concrete, achievable goals ("Use AI to draft one email," "Summarize a document with AI," "Try a new AI tool I haven't used").
- **Lock It:** Confirm goal selection. App will check back on this.
- **XP:** 15 XP (milestone: "Orientation Complete" badge)

## FUNDAMENTALS MODULE F2: "How to Talk to AI"

**Goal:** Prompting is the single most valuable skill. This is the longest fundamentals module because it's the highest-leverage. 8 lessons.

### F2-1: "Your First Real Prompt"
- **Concept:** A prompt is just what you type into an AI. But there's a difference between typing "help me write an email" and giving AI enough context to actually help. The difference is specificity.
- **See It:** Side-by-side — vague prompt and output vs. specific prompt and output. Same task (writing a follow-up email), dramatically different results.
- **Try It:** Given a task (writing a meeting recap email), write a prompt. App rates it on specificity and shows how a more specific version performs.
- **Lock It:** "Which prompt is more likely to give you a useful result?" (two options)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F2-2: "The Anatomy of a Good Prompt"
- **Concept:** Good prompts usually have four parts: Context (who you are, what's the situation), Task (what you want AI to do), Format (how the output should look), Constraints (what to avoid, length, tone).
- **See It:** A prompt broken into its four labeled parts with color coding. Then the AI output it produces.
- **Try It:** Given a jumbled set of prompt pieces, drag them into the right order / category (context, task, format, constraint).
- **Lock It:** "What's missing from this prompt?" (identify the missing component)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F2-3: "Tone and Voice"
- **Concept:** You can tell AI to write in any tone: professional, casual, friendly, firm, empathetic, funny. This is the easiest way to make output feel less "AI-generated" and more like you.
- **See It:** The same email written in three tones — professional, casual, empathetic. Same content, completely different feel.
- **Try It:** Given a scenario (declining a meeting invite), write a prompt specifying tone. Compare output to a prompt without tone specified.
- **Lock It:** "Your manager is upset about a missed deadline. Which tone instruction would work best for your reply?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F2-4: "Give AI Examples"
- **Concept:** One of the most powerful techniques: show AI what you want by giving it an example. "Write a subject line like this: [example]." This is called few-shot prompting, but you don't need to know the name — just the technique.
- **See It:** Prompt without example → generic output. Same prompt with one example → output that matches the style. Night and day difference.
- **Try It:** Given a task (writing product descriptions), craft a prompt that includes an example of the desired style. Rate the output.
- **Lock It:** "When should you include an example in your prompt?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F2-5: "Think Step by Step"
- **Concept:** For complex tasks, telling AI to "think step by step" or breaking the request into stages produces dramatically better results. This works because it forces the AI to reason through the problem instead of jumping to an answer.
- **See It:** A complex task (analyzing a pros/cons list for a business decision) — single prompt vs. "think step by step" prompt. The step-by-step version catches nuances the single prompt misses.
- **Try It:** Given a complex scenario (choosing between two job offers), write a prompt that asks AI to reason through it step by step.
- **Lock It:** "Which type of task benefits MOST from step-by-step prompting?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F2-6: "Iterate, Don't Start Over"
- **Concept:** AI conversations are iterative. You don't need to write the perfect prompt — send something, then refine. "Make it shorter." "More formal." "Add a section about X." This is how power users actually work.
- **See It:** A 4-message conversation: initial prompt → output → "make it more concise and add bullet points" → better output → "change the opening to be more direct" → final output. Show how each step improved it.
- **Try It:** Given an initial AI output that's close but not right, write 2-3 follow-up instructions to improve it. App simulates the refinement.
- **Lock It:** "Your AI output is too long and too formal. What's the best follow-up?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F2-7: "When AI Gets It Wrong"
- **Concept:** AI will confidently state wrong things (hallucinations), make up sources, and produce plausible-sounding nonsense. This isn't a bug you can avoid — it's a feature of how the technology works. Your job is verification, not blind trust.
- **See It:** A realistic example — AI writes a paragraph with a fabricated statistic and a made-up citation. Looks perfectly real. Then the reveal.
- **Try It:** Read three AI-generated paragraphs. Identify which claims need to be verified and which are likely safe. (Not "find the lie" — more "what would you check?")
- **Lock It:** "Which of these AI outputs is safe to use without checking?" (trick question — you should always check key facts)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F2-8: "Your Prompting Cheat Sheet"
- **Concept:** Recap of the module with a printable/saveable reference card. The 5 key techniques: be specific, use the 4-part structure, set tone, give examples, iterate.
- **See It:** The cheat sheet itself — visual, clean, designed to be saved/screenshotted.
- **Try It:** Final challenge: given a complex real-world task, write the best prompt you can using everything from this module. App scores it on all dimensions.
- **Lock It:** Confidence check — "How comfortable are you writing prompts now?" (self-assessment, not a quiz)
- **XP:** 20 XP (milestone: "Prompt Crafter" badge)

## FUNDAMENTALS MODULE F3: "Which Tool Should I Use?"

**Goal:** Clear the decision paralysis. Not an exhaustive review — a decision framework. 5 lessons.

### F3-1: "You Don't Need to Try Everything"
- **Concept:** There are hundreds of AI tools. You need 2-3. The goal isn't to use the best tool for every task — it's to find tools you're comfortable with that cover your main use cases.
- **See It:** A visual showing the "AI tool explosion" — hundreds of logos — then zooming into the 5-6 that matter for most people. Less is more.
- **Try It:** From a list of 10 AI tools, pick the 3 you'd try first based on your role. App explains why each choice makes sense (or doesn't).
- **Lock It:** "What's the biggest risk of trying to learn too many AI tools at once?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F3-2: "Chatbots Compared: ChatGPT vs. Claude vs. Gemini"
- **Concept:** These are the big three. ChatGPT is the most popular and best all-rounder. Claude is strongest for writing, analysis, and long documents. Gemini is best if you're in the Google ecosystem. All are free to start.
- **See It:** Same task run through all three, with a brief comparison of outputs. Not "which is best" but "notice how they're different."
- **Try It:** Three scenarios — for each one, pick which chatbot you'd use and why. App gives the expert recommendation.
- **Lock It:** "Your task involves analyzing a 40-page PDF. Which tool is your best bet?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F3-3: "Beyond Chatbots: Tools That Work Inside Your Workflow"
- **Concept:** Chatbots require copy-pasting. But there are AI tools that live inside the apps you already use — Copilot in Microsoft 365, Gemini in Google Workspace, Notion AI, Grammarly. These are often more practical for daily work because they reduce friction.
- **See It:** Demo of Copilot summarizing an email thread inside Outlook vs. copy-pasting the same thread into ChatGPT. Same result, very different effort.
- **Try It:** Map exercise — "What apps do you use daily?" (pick from list) → app shows which AI integrations exist for each one.
- **Lock It:** "When is an embedded AI tool better than a standalone chatbot?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F3-4: "How to Evaluate a New AI Tool in 5 Minutes"
- **Concept:** A simple framework: (1) What does it do that I can't already do? (2) Is there a free tier? (3) Where does my data go? (4) Does it integrate with what I already use? If you can answer these four questions, you can evaluate any new tool without getting overwhelmed.
- **See It:** Walk through the framework with a real example — evaluating a new AI writing tool that just launched.
- **Try It:** Given a description of a new AI tool, answer the 4 evaluation questions. App scores whether your assessment matches the expert take.
- **Lock It:** "A new AI tool promises to 'revolutionize your workflow.' What's the FIRST question to ask?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F3-5: "Build Your AI Stack"
- **Concept:** Personalized recommendation. Based on your role, your existing tools, and what you've learned — here's your recommended starting stack of 2-3 AI tools.
- **See It:** A personalized "Your AI Stack" card showing recommended tools with one-line reasons.
- **Try It:** "This week's challenge: Try your top recommended tool for one real task. Come back and tell us how it went." (Sets up a follow-up check-in)
- **Lock It:** Confirm stack selection. App saves this for future reference and tracks their journey with these tools.
- **XP:** 15 XP (milestone: "Tool Ready" badge)

## FUNDAMENTALS MODULE F4: "How to Keep Up"

**Goal:** Give them a sustainable system for staying current without drowning. This is about building an ongoing practice, not just finishing a course. 4 lessons.

### F4-1: "Why It Feels Like You Can't Keep Up"
- **Concept:** The AI landscape changes faster than any technology in history. New tools launch weekly. But here's the secret: 90% of launches don't matter. You need a filter, not more information.
- **See It:** Timeline of the last 12 months of AI news. Highlight the 5 things that actually mattered vs. the 50 that didn't. The signal-to-noise ratio is terrible.
- **Try It:** Given 6 recent AI headlines, sort them into "actually matters" vs. "noise." App reveals which ones had lasting impact.
- **Lock It:** "What's the best way to stay current with AI without spending hours reading?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F4-2: "Your AI Information Diet"
- **Concept:** Pick 2-3 sources, check them once a week. That's it. Recommendations by type: one newsletter (The Rundown, Ben's Bites, TLDR AI), one community (relevant subreddit or Discord), one person to follow (pick someone in your industry who covers AI practically).
- **See It:** A curated list of recommended sources by role — not a dump of 50 links, just the top 2-3 for each type.
- **Try It:** "Build your information diet" — pick one newsletter, one community, one person. App saves these as their personal radar.
- **Lock It:** "How often should you check your AI sources to stay current without burning out?" (multiple choice — answer: weekly is enough)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F4-3: "How to Try New Things Without Overwhelm"
- **Concept:** When a new tool or feature launches, you don't need to learn it immediately. The "two-week rule": if people are still talking about it after two weeks, give it 15 minutes. Most things fade. The ones that stick are worth your time.
- **See It:** Example — when Claude Code launched, there was massive hype. Two weeks later, adoption data showed it was real. Contrast with a hyped tool that disappeared.
- **Try It:** Given 4 recent AI launches, apply the two-week rule: which ones are worth trying based on staying power? App reveals the answer.
- **Lock It:** "A colleague says you MUST try this new AI tool that launched yesterday. What's your move?" (multiple choice)
- **XP:** 10 XP

### F4-4: "You're Not Behind — You're Here"
- **Concept:** Reframe the anxiety. You've completed the fundamentals. You know what exists, how to talk to AI, how to pick tools, and how to stay current. That puts you ahead of the vast majority of people. The goal was never to know everything — it was to stop feeling frozen. You're not frozen anymore.
- **See It:** "Your Fluent Profile" — a visual summary of everything they've learned, their AI stack, their goals, their streak. Celebration moment.
- **Try It:** "Set your next goal" — pick a role track to continue with, or explore a tool elective. This is the transition point.
- **Lock It:** No quiz. Just a reflection: "What's one thing you'll do differently this week because of what you learned?" (free text, saved to profile)
- **XP:** 25 XP (milestone: "Fundamentals Complete" badge, major celebration)

## ROLE TRACK: "AI for Office Work" (MVP Track)

**Goal:** Take everything from Fundamentals and apply it to daily office/admin work — email, documents, meetings, data, and scheduling. Each module is a cluster of related use cases. ~20 lessons across 4 modules.

### Module OW-1: "AI for Email & Communication" (5 lessons)

**OW-1-1: "Draft Replies in Seconds"**
- Concept: The basic workflow — paste email, describe desired tone and response, get a draft. When to use AI vs. when to write yourself.
- See It: Three examples — quick acknowledgment, detailed response, difficult conversation. Each with the prompt used.
- Try It: Given an email from a frustrated client, write a prompt to draft a reply. App evaluates and shows a better prompt if needed.
- Lock It: "When should you NOT use AI to reply to an email?" (multiple choice)

**OW-1-2: "Summarize Email Threads"**
- Concept: Long email threads are time vampires. AI can extract the key decisions, open questions, and action items from a 20-message thread in seconds.
- See It: A long email thread → prompt → clean summary with decisions and action items.
- Try It: Given a messy thread, write a summarization prompt. Compare output to the expert prompt version.
- Lock It: "What should you always include when asking AI to summarize an email thread?" (multiple choice)

**OW-1-3: "Write Emails From Scratch"**
- Concept: You don't need to stare at a blank screen. Give AI the context (who, what, why, tone) and let it draft. You edit, you don't write from zero.
- See It: Example — "I need to email my team about the new project timeline. Key points: deadline moved up 2 weeks, need everyone to reprioritize, tone should be urgent but not alarming." → output.
- Try It: Given a scenario, write the prompt for a new email. Rate the output.
- Lock It: Quick match — scenario to prompt approach.

**OW-1-4: "Make Your Writing Sound Like You"**
- Concept: AI output often sounds generic. Two fixes: (1) give it examples of your writing style, (2) always edit the output before sending. The goal is AI-assisted, not AI-written.
- See It: Before/after — generic AI email vs. the same email after feeding in a style example.
- Try It: Write a short paragraph in your natural style. Then ask AI to draft an email matching that style. See how close it gets.
- Lock It: "What's the most effective way to make AI match your writing style?" (multiple choice)

**OW-1-5: "Triage and Prioritize"**
- Concept: You can ask AI to scan a list of emails/tasks and help you prioritize. "Here are 15 things in my inbox. Which 3 need my attention today?"
- See It: A list of 10 emails with brief descriptions → AI prioritization with reasoning.
- Try It: Given a list of tasks/emails, write a prompt to prioritize. Compare your intuition to AI's recommendation.
- Lock It: "What context does AI need to prioritize your inbox effectively?" (multiple choice)
- XP: Module complete badge — "Email Power User"

### Module OW-2: "AI for Documents & Reports" (5 lessons)

**OW-2-1: "First Drafts in Minutes"**
- Concept: The hardest part of writing is starting. AI eliminates the blank page. Give it an outline or key points, get a first draft, then make it yours.
- See It: An outline (5 bullet points for a project update) → prompt → full first draft in 30 seconds.
- Try It: Given bullet points for a report, write a prompt to generate a first draft. Evaluate the output.
- Lock It: "What should your prompt include when asking AI to draft a document?" (multiple choice)

**OW-2-2: "Summarize Long Documents"**
- Concept: Expanding on the quick-start lesson. Different summarization strategies: executive summary, key takeaways, action items, FAQ extraction. Each requires a slightly different prompt.
- See It: Same document, four different summary types. Each one is useful for a different audience.
- Try It: Given a document, write prompts for two different summary types. Compare outputs.
- Lock It: Match summary type to audience (e.g., executive summary → leadership, action items → project team).

**OW-2-3: "Edit and Polish"**
- Concept: AI is an excellent editor. "Make this more concise." "Fix the grammar." "Make this sound more professional." "Restructure this for clarity." Each is a different editing pass.
- See It: A rough paragraph → four different editing prompts → four different improved versions.
- Try It: Given a rough draft, apply three different editing prompts in sequence. See the progressive improvement.
- Lock It: "What type of edit should you do LAST?" (multiple choice — answer: tone/style after structure/clarity)

**OW-2-4: "Tables, Data, and Formatting"**
- Concept: AI can convert unstructured text into tables, format data, and create structured documents. "Turn these meeting notes into a table with columns for Action Item, Owner, Deadline."
- See It: Messy text → prompt → formatted table. Another example: raw data → prompt → organized spreadsheet-ready format.
- Try It: Given unstructured data, write a prompt to structure it. Compare output to the expert version.
- Lock It: "What format instruction gets the best results for structured data?" (multiple choice)

**OW-2-5: "Templates You'll Reuse"**
- Concept: The real power move: save your best prompts as templates. "Weekly update template," "Meeting recap template," "Client proposal template." You build these once, reuse them forever.
- See It: A library of 5 ready-made prompt templates for common office tasks. Each one is copy-paste ready.
- Try It: Create your own prompt template for a task you do regularly. App saves it to "Your Templates."
- Lock It: "What makes a prompt template reusable?" (multiple choice)
- XP: Module complete badge — "Document Master"

### Module OW-3: "AI for Meetings & Collaboration" (5 lessons)

**OW-3-1: "Meeting Prep in 5 Minutes"**
- Concept: Before any meeting, give AI the context (who's attending, agenda, your goals) and ask for prep notes: talking points, questions to ask, potential objections.
- See It: Meeting context → prompt → prep doc with talking points, questions, and a one-line reminder of each attendee's recent concerns.
- Try It: Given a meeting scenario, write a prep prompt. Evaluate the output.
- Lock It: Quiz on what to include in a meeting prep prompt.

**OW-3-2: "Turn Recordings into Action Items"**
- Concept: If you have a meeting transcript (from Otter, Zoom, Teams, etc.), AI can turn it into structured notes instantly. The prompt matters: don't just say "summarize" — ask for decisions, action items, and unresolved questions separately.
- See It: Raw transcript → structured output with three sections.
- Try It: Given a transcript excerpt, write a prompt that extracts action items with owners.
- Lock It: Match prompt type to desired output.

**OW-3-3: "Follow Up Without the Hassle"**
- Concept: After every meeting, there's a follow-up email nobody wants to write. Give AI the meeting notes and ask for a follow-up. Done in 60 seconds.
- See It: Meeting notes → prompt → professional follow-up email with next steps.
- Try It: Write a follow-up prompt given meeting notes. Send-ready in one try.
- Lock It: "What should always be in a post-meeting follow-up?" (multiple choice)

**OW-3-4: "Brainstorm Better"**
- Concept: AI is a surprisingly good brainstorming partner. The trick: ask it to generate ideas you wouldn't think of. "Give me 10 solutions to X, including at least 3 unconventional ones."
- See It: A brainstorming session — the user's 3 ideas vs. AI's 10 (including creative ones the user wouldn't have considered).
- Try It: Given a real workplace challenge, brainstorm with AI. Write progressively better prompts to push past obvious ideas.
- Lock It: "What prompt technique gets the most creative brainstorming results?" (multiple choice)

**OW-3-5: "Presentations and Decks"**
- Concept: AI can outline presentations, write speaker notes, and draft slide content. It can't design slides, but it can give you the structure and words.
- See It: A brief → prompt → presentation outline with slide titles, key points per slide, and speaker notes.
- Try It: Given a presentation topic and audience, write a prompt for an outline. Evaluate the structure.
- Lock It: "What's the best way to use AI for a presentation?" (multiple choice — answer: outline and content, not design)
- XP: Module complete badge — "Meeting Pro"

### Module OW-4: "AI for Data & Organization" (5 lessons)

**OW-4-1: "Make Sense of Spreadsheet Data"**
- Concept: You can paste spreadsheet data into AI and ask questions about it. "What trends do you see?" "Which month had the highest growth?" "Are there any anomalies?" No formulas needed.
- See It: A table of monthly sales data → questions → AI insights with explanations.
- Try It: Given a data table, ask three different questions. Evaluate which AI answers are trustworthy and which need verification.
- Lock It: "What should you always do after AI analyzes your data?" (multiple choice — answer: verify key numbers)

**OW-4-2: "Write Formulas You Don't Know"**
- Concept: Instead of Googling Excel formulas, ask AI. "I need a formula that looks up a value in column B based on a match in column A." AI writes the formula and explains it.
- See It: Three formula requests → AI responses with formulas AND explanations of how they work.
- Try It: Given a spreadsheet scenario, describe what you need in plain English. AI generates the formula. User checks if it's right.
- Lock It: "What should you include when asking AI to write a spreadsheet formula?" (multiple choice)

**OW-4-3: "Organize Your Digital Life"**
- Concept: AI can help you create organizational systems. "Here are 200 files in my downloads folder. Suggest a folder structure." "Categorize these bookmarks." "Create a naming convention for my project files."
- See It: A chaotic file list → prompt → organized folder structure with reasoning.
- Try It: Describe a real organizational challenge. Get AI's suggestion. Evaluate it.
- Lock It: "When is AI-suggested organization most useful?" (multiple choice)

**OW-4-4: "Research Without Going Down Rabbit Holes"**
- Concept: AI can give you a structured research starting point. Instead of opening 30 tabs, ask: "What are the key considerations for [topic]? Give me the top 5 factors with a one-sentence explanation of each."
- See It: A research question → structured overview → then how to go deeper on the parts that matter.
- Try It: Given a work research task, write a prompt that gets you from zero to informed in one response.
- Lock It: "What's the risk of using AI as your only research source?" (multiple choice — answer: hallucinations, need to verify)

**OW-4-5: "Your AI Workflow (Putting It All Together)"**
- Concept: Final lesson of the track. Review what they've learned, identify the 3-5 AI use cases they'll actually use going forward, and set up their personal workflow.
- See It: A "Your AI Workflow" dashboard showing their saved templates, preferred tools, and most-used techniques.
- Try It: Design your daily AI workflow: "What will you use AI for every day? What's weekly? What's as-needed?" Build a personal AI playbook.
- Lock It: Reflection — "What's the single biggest way AI has changed how you work?" (free text, saved)
- XP: 30 XP (milestone: "Office AI Expert" badge, major celebration)

**Role Track Complete.** The user has gone from frozen to having a practiced, personal AI workflow for their daily work. They've written dozens of prompts, evaluated AI outputs, built templates, and made AI a habit.
